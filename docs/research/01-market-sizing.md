# 01 — Market sizing and willingness to pay

**Question** — How large is the realistically addressable population of US parents (kids of any age, tech- and privacy-savvy, currently sharing via Google Photos / WhatsApp groups) who would pay for a dedicated family photo-sharing subscription, and what is the evidence on their willingness to pay?

**Context** — The brief (`brief §Business model`) assumes a paid-only family subscription at ~$5–8/mo or ~$50–70/yr with no free tier, and a success bar of $3–5k/month net profit (`brief §Success bar`). The persona is US parents of kids of *any* age (`brief §Persona`). Whether enough paying households exist, at that price, outside the 0–5 band, decides whether a solo builder can reach the bar.

**Method caveat** — WebSearch tool was broken for the whole session (API 400 on every call); DuckDuckGo/Bing scraping worked briefly then rate-limited. Research therefore relied on direct fetches of known primary URLs, Census bulk files (`www2.census.gov` xls/csv), ASX announcement PDFs (via AFR mirror), MIXI IR PDFs (via irpocket feed), Apple App Store listings, and the AARP PDF. Not reached: Census CPS "C3" children-by-age tables (WAF-blocked), Chatbooks pricing (JS-rendered), Tinybeans MAU/geographic split by country, MIXI FamilyAlbum *Premium* subscriber count (not disclosed anywhere found), any US-specific survey on how sharing frequency changes with child age (searched Pew, Mott Poll, CCO UK; none break down by child age), Google Photos US-only user count (only global figures exist). Overrun: ~20 sources and ~3,000 words vs the ~15/~1,500 guide. Justification: the ticket has five sub-questions each needing its own primary source (Census bulk tables, two Pew fact sheets, AARP PDF, six competitor price pages, five ASX/MIXI filings), and price rows must each carry a retrieval date. Tables, not prose, account for most of the length.

## Conclusion

The addressable population is large in raw terms but the *paying* population for this exact niche is demonstrably small, and the best-run US-centric incumbent monetises it at roughly $75/yr per payer with ~90–95k payers worldwide after 13 years and an acquisition. Concretely: 33.6M US families have own children under 18 (**VERIFIED**, Census CPS 2025), 96–97% of parent-age adults own smartphones (**VERIFIED**, Pew 2025), and only 30–40% of US adults 30–49 use WhatsApp at all (**VERIFIED**, Pew 2025) — so the brief's "currently on WhatsApp groups" filter already cuts the base by more than half. Paid conversion is the binding constraint: Tinybeans, with ~0.86M MAU and >1M free users, had 51k paid subscribers at FY25 (about 6% of MAU, **INFERRED** ratio from two VERIFIED numbers) at ARPU US$75/yr; FamilyAlbum has 30M registered / 12M MAU users globally but MIXI does not disclose Premium subscriber counts, and its whole Lifestyle segment (FamilyAlbum plus GPS tags, prints, New Year cards, ads, and the minimo salon app) made ¥17.2bn in FY2026 (~US$115m, **INFERRED** at ¥150/$, see A6) with EBITDA only just turning positive. Willingness to pay clusters at $60–75/yr for the category (FamilyAlbum Premium Family $59/yr, Tinybeans+ $74.99/yr) and $12–36/yr for general cloud storage (iCloud+ 50–200GB, Google One 100GB) — the brief's $50–70/yr band sits exactly where incumbents already price, so there is no price umbrella to hide under. On the age question: every incumbent's positioning, App Store naming ("Share *Baby* Photos"), acquisition channel (Babylist, expecting-mum bags) and MIXI's own KPI ("65% of parents in Japan use it when children are born") point to demand entering at birth; no source was found showing sustained paid demand from parents of school-age or teen children (**NO EVIDENCE FOUND**), and Tinybeans' 95% monthly retention implies a 20-month mean paid life, i.e. most payers churn out well before a child is 5.

**Verdict impact: supports don't-build** on the brief's current premise (any-age persona, paid-only from launch, $3–5k/mo net). The population exists, but the evidence says the payer pool for this niche is tens of thousands of households globally per incumbent, entered at birth, at ~$6/mo — and it took the two survivors a decade, venture/public capital, and adjacent revenue (prints, GPS, ads, e-commerce) to reach break-even. The $3–5k/mo bar is reachable in principle (~600–1,000 payers at $60–75/yr, **INFERRED** arithmetic, not a forecast) but only if 03 and the distribution gap-fill show an acquisition channel that a solo builder can run at Tinybeans-like CAC ($19–35) and if the any-age persona is narrowed.

## Implications for tickets 02, 03, 04, 06, X1, X4, X5

- **03 (competitors)**: verify FamilyAlbum's *free* tier really is unlimited storage + 11 prints/mo in the US (VERIFIED on family-album.com; re-check in-app) — a paid-only entrant at $5.99 must beat a free product with 372k US ratings. Extend the ceiling table with Qeepsake, Notabli, Backpack, Kinzoo, Famm; look for any US paid-only family album product that survived.
- **X1 + fog (distribution channel)**: Tinybeans CAC US$19–35 and channel = expecting-parent registries (Babylist). Test whether a solo builder can access any birth-moment channel; if not, A3's trial volumes are unreachable. Also test the WhatsApp-share assumption (A1) against actual US family-chat channel mix (iMessage/Facebook Messenger dominate per Pew 2025).
- **Fog (pricing point), fed by 03 and 06**: brief's $5–8/mo // $50–70/yr is not a differentiated price; it matches FamilyAlbum Family ($5.99/$59) and undercuts Tinybeans+ ($7.99/$74.99). Need to decide: price parity with a paid-only handicap, or a premium ($10–12/mo, Family Pro / Tinybeans Family territory) justified by self-hosting/print-prep. Resolve A2 (share already paying for storage).
- **06 (unit economics)**: convert A5 into a real model — store fees, storage/egress for multi-attendee event pooling (video heavy), payer life ~20 months (95% retention) unless A4 is disproven. Check whether $3–5k/mo net is reachable at <1,500 payers.
- **X5 (grandparent role)**: AARP 2019 — 24% of grandparents video-chat "often", 28% text often, 40% say they're tech-savvy, 78% of 65+ own smartphones (Pew 2025). So ~1 in 4 grandparents is a natural digital participant; the other 3 are the adoption-friction population. Verify whether FamilyAlbum's 12M MAU includes grandparent viewers (MIXI now reports comments/stickers growth as a family-communication KPI).
- **X4 (review mining)**: prioritise finding evidence on A4 — do reviewers with kids 6+ still upload / still pay? Tinybeans' Legacy tier reviews and FamilyAlbum reviews mentioning "school age" are the cheapest test.
- **Gap-fill candidates**: (a) US-specific data on photo-sharing frequency by child age (none found); (b) FamilyAlbum Premium subscriber count or Premium revenue (MIXI Q&A transcript or Japanese analyst notes); (c) US paid iCloud+/Google One penetration among parents; (d) Chatbooks Monthbooks price and subscriber count.
